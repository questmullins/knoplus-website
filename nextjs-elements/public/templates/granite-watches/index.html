Reference notes

Four shapes. Four turning points.

The page is less a product grid than a guided reading: a place to explain how design language, engineering, and culture slowly turn objects into reference points.

[01]

Royal Oak

In the early 1970s, steel stopped behaving like the humble option. The integrated bracelet, exposed geometry, and industrial finish made the sports watch feel architectural.

Industrial luxury// Steel as statement

[02]

Nautilus

The porthole shape softened the idea of a luxury sports watch. It felt nautical, relaxed, and expensive without needing to dress itself in precious metal first.

Nautical form// Casual prestige
Read History
[03]

Daytona

The chronograph carries the memory of measurement: speed, laps, tension, and proof. Its history is tied to sport because it made elapsed time visible.

Measured speed// Chronograph culture
Read History
[04]

Calatrava

The dress watch is a lesson in restraint. Thin cases, clear dials, and careful proportion make the object disappear until someone knows what to look for.

Formal restraint// Proportion first
